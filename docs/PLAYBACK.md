# Playback internals

Non-obvious behavior in `lib/services/audio_player_service.dart` and `packages/voidweaver_player`. Keep it in mind when changing either.

The player is `VoidweaverPlayer` from the local plugin `packages/voidweaver_player`: a thin layer over Media3 ExoPlayer (`PlaylistPlayer.java`) with a gain stage per item (`GainAudioProcessor.java`) and a Media3 media session for the system controls. It replaced `just_audio`, which had no way to apply a gain per track, and `audio_service`.

## The player owns the queue

The worker (below) hands the whole playlist to the player with `setAudioSources()`. The player moves from track to track by itself, gaplessly, and the service follows its current index. There is no completion logic of its own in the service: `ProcessingState.completed` only means the end of the playlist.

At the end of the playlist the player stays `completed` with `playing` still set. The system controls show that as playing, and `play()` does nothing in that state. `_onQueueCompleted()` therefore pauses the player at the start of the last track. Play replays it as a new play: it gets a start time and "now playing" on `play()`, like a restored song, and is scrobbled again. The completed event that follows the pause has `playing` false and is ignored.

Earlier versions loaded one track at a time and advanced on `completed`. That event sometimes didn't fire, which needed fallbacks (near the end, stuck playhead, paused near the end), which in turn caused double skips and skips on pause. Don't bring them back: within a sequence the player advances without them.

- Every source is tagged with a `_QueueEntry` (generation and playlist index). `_followPlayer()` maps the player's current index to its entry, so a sequence position always resolves to the right song. Entries of an older generation (a replaced sequence) are ignored.
- Only an advance by one counts as the player moving on (`_followPlayer()`): it scrobbles the finished track if it was heard past the threshold, then sends "now playing", and starts the next downloads. Index events can repeat or arrive late, so repeated and backwards events are ignored.
- Changes to the player's index, sequence or position go through the worker (see [One worker for the player](#one-worker-for-the-player)), one player call at a time. Index events are ignored while it runs, and the service catches up with the player afterwards (`_work()`).
- After a move's seek, `_awaitedIndex` holds the target until the player reports it. Until then an event can still be from before the seek; after Previous, the old track's index would look like an advance and the target would be scrobbled. The worker leaves index events alone while it runs, which covers the time before the seek (the target's ReplayGain read, a reload): a natural advance of the track that's still playing then is neither followed nor scrobbled. `_awaitedIndex` covers the events that arrive late, after the worker is done. If the player passed the target before reporting it (a track shorter than the seek took), it stays set until the player reports the target or the next move clears it.
- The end of the queue (`completed`) is only handled while the worker isn't running and the player is on the service's current track (`_onPlayerCompleted()`); one reported while it runs is checked again after it. Otherwise an end in flight while a skip already made its target current (reading its ReplayGain before the seek), or while a new queue is being prepared, was taken for that track's end: it was scrobbled without being heard. The finished track then isn't credited with its last seconds; a skip has already scrobbled it if it passed the threshold.

## One worker for the player

Every change of the player's track, sequence or position runs in one worker (`_work()`), one player call at a time. Commands don't call the player themselves for that (play, pause, stop and the volume still go straight to it); they only say what they want:

- **A move** (`_Move`, at most one, `_move`): the track the player should end up on, where in it, and whether it starts there. Starting an album, random songs or a song, a skip, Play when the player holds nothing to play, restoring the queue, and the recovery after a load error are all moves. `_requestMove()` replaces the move in progress, which completes as superseded; the new track becomes current at once, so the app shows it while it loads.
- **Queued operations** (`_ops`): seeks within the current track (`seekTo()`) and the rewind at the end of the queue. They run after the move. A seek is dropped if the track changed before it ran.

The worker takes the move step by step (`_step()`): read the target's ReplayGain, then load the queue at the target or seek to it, then start it unless paused (`_finish()`, `_startUnlessPaused()`). After every await it checks that the move is still the requested one with the same target (`_Move.version`), and otherwise starts over with what is requested now. The ReplayGain read and the cache lookup after a failure don't hold up a newer request (`_orChange()`); a load or seek the player is carrying out runs to its end before the next one.

The worker never compares the player with what was asked for and corrects it. What the player does on its own (pausing for audio focus or unplugged headphones, moving on at the end of a track, completing, failing) is handled by the event handlers, and only commands and errors request moves.

Whether a move loads or seeks is decided right before the player call (`_needsLoad`): it loads if the player holds another playlist's sequence or none, or is idle (after `stop()` or an error, where a seek moves to an item without loading it). So a skip while a new queue is still being prepared loads the new queue at the skip's target, and a skip or Play after an error loads the queue again.

Index events and `completed` are left alone while the worker runs; it catches up afterwards (`_followPlayer()`, `_onPlayerCompleted()`), and handles the errors that arrived meanwhile (see [Load errors](#load-errors)).

The test getters: `startsInProgress` is 1 while a move that was asked to start playback is in progress, `pendingPlayerOps` is non-zero while the worker runs (index events and the end of the queue wait for it), `needsSourceReload` is `_needsLoad`, and `isSkipOperationInProgress` is true while the move is a skip.

## ReplayGain per track

Every item in the sequence carries its own gain (`AudioSource.gain`, from `_gainFor()`, which asks `ReplayGainResolver`). On the native side, `GainAudioProcessor` sits in ExoPlayer's audio sink and multiplies the decoded samples by the gain of the item they belong to. The sink drains its processors and flushes them with the next item's `StreamMetadata` exactly where one item's samples end, so the gain changes at the sample where the track changes, also during a gapless transition. Nothing is applied when the player reports the new index; that event arrives a few hundred ms later, after the new track is already audible.

- Gains can be above 1 (quiet tracks, positive preamp); samples are clipped at full scale. With clipping prevention on, the peak keeps them below that.
- A gain read later (from a downloaded file, or by a range request) is set on the song's items with `_updateGains()`. The start track's gain is read before the queue is loaded, and a skip's target before seeking.
- Changed settings apply to every item at once (`refreshReplayGainVolume()`).
- Each switch to a new item is logged with the tag `VoidweaverGain` (`adb logcat -s VoidweaverGain`).
- Track mode still changes the level between songs that run into each other (live albums, mixes), since each has its own gain. Album mode keeps one gain within an album. Smart mode picks between them by the queue's source (`_playlistSource == QueueSource.album`, also after a restore).

## Downloads and the cache

Every item in the sequence is a stream URL with a cache key (server and song id, `AudioCache.keyFor()`). The player reads through its Media3 cache, so a cached track plays without the network wherever it is in the queue, and the track that's playing is cached as it streams. While a track plays, the next 3 are downloaded into the same cache (see [CACHING.md](CACHING.md)). The queue itself never changes for that; earlier versions swapped streams for downloaded files in the sequence, which needed care around the track the player was about to move to.

## Load errors

The player retries a failed load 8 times, about 25 s in all (`LOAD_RETRY_COUNT` in `LoadErrorPolicy`), before it reports an error. With ExoPlayer's default of a few seconds, a short network drop while it loaded the next track (near the end of the current one) became a load error: ExoPlayer cut the current track off, moved to the failed one and stopped there (0:00/0:00). A response that's a document instead of audio (a Subsonic error such as an unknown song or a wrong password, or a reverse proxy's page, see [CACHING.md](CACHING.md)) is reported at once: the server would give the same answer again, so retries would only hold up playback for those 25 s, and with a password changed on the server, each track that isn't cached would cost 9 failed logins. Downloads ahead are retried too, see [CACHING.md](CACHING.md).

A track that can't load (offline and not downloaded, or a stream breaking off) is reported on `errorStream`; the player is idle afterwards. `_onPlayerError()` requests a move with the failure: it looks for the next completely cached track (`_nextCachedIndex()`, which asks the cache) and goes on there if playback was meant to run. Otherwise, or if there's none, playback stops on the failing track (`_finish()`), and the next `play()` loads the queue again from there. A move whose own target fails (`setAudioSources()` throws, or its seek ends with the player idle and the error held, below) goes on with the same search.

Which track an error is about:

- An error of an item of an older generation (the tag of `sequence[error.index]`), a sequence that a load is replacing (still reading its start track's ReplayGain), is dropped: the new sequence is loaded anyway. Before, it stopped the new queue's start track with a message for a track that never played. An error without an item (no index, or past the end) counts as the current sequence's. That this works for errors from the native side relies on the ordering: Dart replaces `sequence` when it calls `setAudioSources()`, errors arriving before its result are ignored (`_settingSources`), and ExoPlayer drops playback info from before `setMediaItems()` while operations are unacknowledged. Without `_settingSources`, an error of the replaced sequence arriving during the load has an index past the end of the new one, counts as current, and the new queue's failure is reported twice.
- An error that arrives while the worker runs is held (`_heldError`). The move's next player call supersedes it: that call loads or seeks anew, so the error must neither stop the new target nor mark the healthy new queue to be loaded again from the start (which Play once did). That covers the track that was playing breaking off while the target's ReplayGain was read (the player is idle then, so the move loads instead of seeking), and the error of a track a skip left. If the call was a seek and the player is idle afterwards, the held error is the target's own failure, unless it's about another item: the track the skip left, whose error (typically at the end of its retries, while it stalled, which is when users press Next) was on its way when the seek was issued. The player is idle because of that one, and a seek on an idle player moves without loading, so the target wasn't tried: the move loads it, with no message. An error still held when the worker is done is handled then.
- When a skip's target fails after presses during the skip moved it on, it goes on to where they point, and the failure isn't reported (`_Move.nextIndex`).

A failure that a newer request superseded is never reported: the request replaces the move (or moves its target), and the step that saw the failure finds its move changed and returns. Only the move that ends on the failed track reports it, once: a start throws it, everything else puts it on `playbackErrors` (`_OnFailure`). A restore that can't load stays quiet and waits for Play, unless Play was pressed while it loaded (see [Restoring playback](#restoring-playback)).

The search for a cached track gives up when a load or skip took over during the cache lookup (the move was replaced or its target moved), not on a seek: a seek only changes where the move starts. A seek meanwhile (the seek bar dragged while it buffered) once left the failure unhandled, shown as loading for good. A pause during the lookup counts as a failure while paused: playback stops on the failed track, reported. After a Stop (before or during the lookup) there's no message. The search isn't part of a skip any more (`_Move.skip` is cleared when the skip's target fails): a Next during it starts a new skip from the failed track.

The player's `seek()` completes once the target is ready or has failed (the error arrives on `errorStream` first). With `just_audio`, a seek to a target that failed never completed; awaited in the worker, that would block every later change. `_seekToItem()` still stops waiting once the player reports an error or goes idle, so a seek that never completes can't block playback again. `setAudioSources()` completes once the start item is ready and throws its own errors, which the move handles the same way.

After `stop()` or an error the player is idle, so `play()` and skips load the queue again instead of seeking (`_needsLoad`), `play()` at `_pendingResumePosition`: the start of the track after an error or a stop, the saved position after a restore that couldn't load.

A password changed on the server and updated in the app (`AppState.updatePassword()`) doesn't reload the queue: playback goes on, as cached tracks play from the cache whatever their URL. The items' stream URLs still carry the old password, though, so `credentialsChanged()` marks them stale (`_sourcesStale`) and starts the downloads of the upcoming tracks again. An item that fails while they're stale (one that wasn't downloaded in time) loads the queue again at that track, with new URLs, instead of skipping it; a failure after that is handled as above. A skip (or any other move, see `_step()`) loads the queue with new URLs too instead of seeking within the stale sequence, and a seek that fails after the password changed while it ran is retried as such a load.

Starting an album or random songs (`_startQueue()`) fetches the songs before it touches the queue. If that fails (offline, an album that isn't cached, no random songs), the current queue plays on, or stays paused, and the state shown while fetching goes back to what it was, unless the player reported a newer one meanwhile. Only a failure after the new queue was handed to the player stops playback (`_onLoadFailed()`). When several starts overlap, the last one wins: songs that arrive for an earlier one are dropped (`_queueRequests`).

The user is told why playback stopped. A start (an album, random songs, a song) throws, and the screen that started it shows a snack bar. Every other stop for a load error (after Play, a skip, a system control, or a track failing while it plays, with no later track downloaded) has no caller to show it, so `_onLoadFailed()` puts a message on `playbackErrors`. `PlaybackErrorListener` (around the home screen) shows it as a snack bar on whatever screen is open, replacing the ones before; one that comes while the app is hidden shows once it's back. `NetworkErrorHelper` explains the player's errors by their Media3 error code (no connection, timeout, HTTP error, a file that can't be played). Continuing at a downloaded track isn't reported.

## Pausing while a track loads

While a track loads or buffers, `playbackState` is `loading`, and the play button stays usable with a spinner around it; `playWhenReady` decides between Pause and Play. While a move is in progress, `playWhenReady` is `_playPending`: whether the move starts its target once it's ready (`_startUnlessPaused()`). `pause()` clears it, `play()` during the move sets it again instead of loading anew. A track that was paused while it loaded stays paused and gets its start time on `play()`, like a restored song. A load error while paused doesn't continue at a downloaded track: playback stops on the failed one.

A skip during a load replaces the load's move, so only the skip starts playback, on its own target, once the player is there. Before the worker, a load that a skip had queued behind announced the skip's target as playing ("now playing", while the player was still on the load's track) and started it before the skip had seeked.

`stop()` clears `_playPending` and marks the move in progress (`_Move.stopped`); the move checks the mark at its next step (`_stopEnds()`). A load (a start, Play after an error, a restore, error recovery) then ends without loading, so the stop sticks: the player stays idle, the notification stays gone, and `play()` loads the track again. A load the stop interrupted (`PlayerInterruptedException`) isn't a failure. A skip reaches its target, paused, and a failure there is reported. A `play()` right after `stop()`, before the move has noticed the stop, sets `_playPending` again, and the move loads and starts its target. Before, a `play()` in between left playback to the load, which then returned without starting.

A move never takes an idle player without an error for a stop: only the mark does. A load whose start item fails right after it's ready can leave the player idle with its error ignored (it came before the load's result, see `_settingSources`); that is a failure of the target, reported like any other. Loading again would repeat it for as long as the stream breaks the same way. Because the move may see the failure before its report on `errorStream` arrives, it remembers the item it failed on (`_failedItem`) until its next player call, and drops a report of that item: the failure is reported once, by the move.

A new playlist takes a new generation as soon as it's set (`_setPlaylist()`), before it's loaded: the player's sequence belongs to the old one from then on, also if the new one never gets loaded (stopped during its load). Otherwise an index event of the old sequence was taken for an advance in the new playlist, announcing a track that wasn't playing.

## Skips

- `next()` and `previous()` seek within the sequence. Nothing is reloaded, and a cached target plays from the cache. While a load hasn't handed its sequence to the player yet (it reads the start track's ReplayGain first, which takes a few seconds when the server sends none), the player still holds the previous sequence, so a skip then loads the queue at its target instead of seeking (`_needsLoad`: the player holds another playlist's sequence). Before, it seeked in the old sequence and the load then started at index 0, with the service showing (and scrobbling) the skip's target. The read comes before the player call on purpose: `setAudioSources()` leaves `playWhenReady` as it was, so with the read after it, the start track of a queue the user had already skipped past would play aloud until the skip's own read and seek, and a seek would show and play from the old start position first. A seek before the move's player call becomes the position it starts at (`_Move.position`); one after it is done once the target is ready (`_Move.seekAfter`).
- Previous restarts the current track once it has played for more than 3 s (`_restartThreshold`), and always on the first track; otherwise it goes back a track. A restart is a new play, like any other skip.
- Presses during a skip are coalesced: a skip in progress continues to the target that later presses move (`_Move.nextIndex`), counting from that target rather than the current track, once it has reached the earlier one (which it doesn't announce: no "now playing", downloads or save; if it was playing, the player keeps playing across the seeks, so it's briefly audible). Pressing Next three times moves three tracks, however long each takes to load; earlier versions dropped presses while a skip ran. The skip buttons stay enabled during a skip for the same reason. Only the press that starts a skip sets `_playPending`; moving its target keeps it, so a Pause or Stop during a skip holds until it reaches its last target, and a Play after it starts that target again.
- Skips within 100 ms of the previous one are ignored (`_skipDebounceMs`) as doubled events. The service does this, so UI and system controls share it.
- A skip scrobbles the current track if it passed the threshold.

There is deliberately no handling for the player pausing near the end of a track: the player keeps `playing` true while buffering and on completion, so ready and not playing only happens after `pause()`.

## Bluetooth and system controls

The plugin's Media3 `MediaSession` drives the notification, the lock screen player and Bluetooth/media button controls (`PlaybackService`, a `MediaSessionService`, hosts it; Media3 builds the notification and keeps the service in the foreground while playing). The session shows the ExoPlayer's real state and the metadata of the current queue item (`AudioSource.title` etc.), so it follows gapless transitions by itself.

- **Commands go to Dart.** The session talks to `SessionPlayer`, which forwards every command (play, pause, stop, next, previous, seek) to Dart instead of acting on the ExoPlayer. `AudioPlayerService` handles them (`_onSystemCommand()`) with the same methods as the app's own buttons, so skips are debounced, scrobbled and reload the queue after an error like any other. Only these commands are available to controllers; nobody else can change the queue.
- **Every controller gets full control** (`PlayerSession.Callback`). By default Media3 gives untrusted controllers read access only, and it can't verify apps that package visibility hides from this one, such as launchers with a media widget (Niagara): they showed the track but their buttons did nothing. With only transport controls on offer, a controller can't do more than a Bluetooth button. The library commands are granted too, for System UI's resumption controls (below); the library itself is empty.
- **Artwork URLs stay private.** The session publishes each item's artwork URI to every controller (as `ART_URI` and the like in the legacy metadata, readable by any notification listener), and cover art URLs carry the Subsonic token and salt, which can be replayed. So the session only gets `voidweaver-art://<item id>`; `ArtworkLoader`, the session's bitmap loader, maps it to the real URL and controllers get the bitmap.
- **Load errors don't end the session.** The ExoPlayer is idle after every load error, also when the service recovers by continuing at a downloaded track or waits for Play to load again. An idle session would remove the notification and stop the foreground service. So `SessionPlayer` reports an idle player as ready (paused, without the error) unless playback was stopped. Only `stop()` ends the notification (`SHOW_NOTIFICATION_FOR_IDLE_PLAYER_NEVER`), and the next play brings it back.
- **One session per player.** Each `VoidweaverPlayer` (one per login) has its own session (built by `PlayerSession`, a `MediaLibrarySession`); disposing it on logout releases the session and removes the notification. `PlaybackService` stops once no session is left.
- **Media keys bypass Flutter while the app is shown.** Android gives key events to the focused window first and passes media keys to the media session only if the app leaves them unhandled. Once a text field had focus (the login form, search), the `FlutterView` keeps the view focus and claims every key, re-sending unhandled ones only within the app, so media buttons (also Bluetooth) did nothing while the app was in the foreground. `MainActivity.dispatchKeyEvent()` hands media keys to `AudioManager.dispatchMediaKeyEvent()` instead, the same path they take when the app isn't focused.
- **The Dart engine outlives the activity.** Playlist logic, scrobbling and downloads run in Dart, so `MainActivity` keeps its `FlutterEngine` in `FlutterEngineCache`. Closing the activity (Back, swiping it from the recents) leaves the engine running; a new activity attaches to it.
- **Swiped away while paused**, Media3 stops `PlaybackService`. The session stays, so media buttons still reach it, and the next play starts the service again (`PlaybackService.ensureStarted()`).
- **Playback resumes after the process has died.** A Play media button (also Bluetooth or a headset) then reaches Media3's `MediaButtonReceiver`, which starts `PlaybackService` in the foreground. Media3 needs a session for it right away, before Dart runs, so `onGetSession()` answers with a stand-in, `ResumePlayer`. It's idle until Play (so the system UI binding at boot shows no notification), then buffering with a "Resuming…" notification, and it starts the cached engine (`AppEngine`, which `MainActivity` uses too). Dart starts as usual. Once `AppState.configure()` has set up the services, it restores the queue in the background (the home screen doesn't wait for it, nor for the album list) and calls `AudioPlayerService.resumeIfRequested()`, which asks the plugin (`takeResumeRequest()`) and plays. The app's player doesn't get a session of its own then: it claims the stand-in's (`PlaybackService.claimResumeSession()`) and takes it over with `MediaSession.setPlayer()` once it plays, or once Dart answers that no resume is pending, or after 60 s (e.g. offline with nothing cached). Until then the stand-in keeps the notification and the foreground service. The session must stay: Android sends media buttons to one session of the app that played last, and when that session is released, it doesn't pick another session of the app. With a separate stand-in that was released, media buttons (also Bluetooth) went to `MediaButtonReceiver` instead, which ignores everything but Play while the app runs, so Pause and the skips did nothing until the next restart. If no player claimed the stand-in (logged out), it's released. Only Play, Play/Pause and the headset button start the app (Android 8+ requires a foreground start); other keys do nothing while it isn't running.
- **System UI's resumption controls.** System UI removes a paused media player after about 10 minutes, and Media3 lets the service leave the foreground after the same time, so the process may be killed afterwards. System UI keeps a player that can be resumed in its place (also after a reboot) only for an app whose service it can browse for the recent track. So `PlaybackService` is a `MediaLibraryService` (the manifest declares `MediaLibraryService` and `MediaBrowserService`), and Media3 answers System UI's request for the recent root by itself. While the session's player is idle (the app isn't running, or nothing is loaded), it asks `onPlaybackResumption()` for the track, which comes from `RecentItem`: the title, artist and album of the current item, saved natively on every track change and cleared when the player is disposed (logout). No artwork, as its URL carries the credentials. Play on these controls goes to the running session, or starts the app through the stand-in as for a media button. Before, System UI couldn't browse the service (`Cannot resume with …PlaybackService` in logcat) and dropped the player instead: no controls in the shade or on the lock screen until the app was opened.

## Audio focus

ExoPlayer owns audio focus (`setAudioAttributes(..., handleAudioFocus = true)` in `PlaylistPlayer`). It pauses on interruptions and resumes after transient ones; while focus is lost for a moment the player reports `playing` false. It also pauses when headphones are unplugged (`setHandleAudioBecomingNoisy`).

Don't request focus anywhere else, including native code. Android treats each focus listener as a separate client. An earlier `voidweaver/audio_focus` channel in `MainActivity` pushed the player's listener down the stack, so when an interruption ended Android notified the wrong listener and playback never resumed.

## Sleep timer

The sleep timer (`startSleepTimer()`, implemented in `SleepTimer`) pauses playback when it runs out. Over its last 30 s (`SleepTimer.fadeDuration`, or the whole timer if it's shorter) it fades the volume out, so the music doesn't stop mid-phrase.

- The fade uses the player's volume (`VoidweaverPlayer.setVolume()`, ExoPlayer's `setVolume()`), not the item gains: it has to carry on across a track change, and it applies on top of the ReplayGain gains. The device volume is left alone.
- Every 250 ms the volume is set from the time left on the timer's clock, so the fade ends with the timer even if steps run late. The curve is cubic: loudness follows the level in dB, so a linear ramp would sound almost unchanged until the last few seconds.
- At the end the volume goes to 0, the player pauses, and the volume goes back to 1 for the next play. The player handles its calls in order, so the pause is never heard at full volume.
- Cancelling, extending or starting a new timer during the fade puts the volume back to 1 right away.

## Restoring playback

`PlaybackPersistenceService` saves the queue, index and position (throttled to every 5 s while playing, and after every track change, seek and pause, so a seek made while paused is kept too; a pause writes it at once). The position goes to its own file, `playback_position.json` in the app's private storage, written to a temporary name and renamed, one write after the other. Android's SharedPreferences rewrites its whole XML file on every change, queue, pending scrobbles and settings included, so a save every 5 s there would rewrite all of it. The queue stays in SharedPreferences and is only written again when it changed: a new playlist list, or ReplayGain read from a file into one of its songs (`queueChanged()`), so a restored queue keeps the gains that were read. On start, `restorePlaybackState()` loads the saved song paused. It runs while the home screen is already shown; if the user starts playback first, the restore gives up and leaves the new queue alone.

- A position file that's missing or can't be read still restores the queue, from its first song. A position saved by an earlier version (SharedPreferences key `playbackPosition`) is read when there's no file, and removed with the next save. A logout deletes the file with the queue.
- The queue is loaded with `initialIndex` and `initialPosition` instead of a `seek()` afterwards. With a separate seek, load events reporting position 0 could arrive after the seek, so the playhead showed 0:00 while audio resumed at the saved position. `_currentPosition` is also set directly so the UI is right before the player reports anything.
- If loading fails (e.g. offline at startup), the restore stays quiet: the player is idle, and `play()` loads the queue again at the restored position (`_pendingResumePosition`). Restoring never skips ahead to a downloaded track, unless Play was pressed while it loaded: then a failure is handled as for any start that plays. Playback continues at the next downloaded track or, with none, stops on the restored song and puts the error on `playbackErrors`; `_pendingResumePosition` keeps the restored position then, so Play tries again there.
- A restored song has no start time (`ListeningTracker.hasStarted`) until it's played. The first `play()` sets it, sends "now playing" and starts the downloads. Without a start time it would never be scrobbled.

## Position updates while hidden

`PlaylistPlayer` sends a state snapshot to Dart every 200 ms while playing, and each one wakes Dart. While the app is hidden (playing with the screen off) nobody sees the position, so `AppState.setVisible()` (driven by `AppRouter`'s lifecycle listener) tells the service, which sets the interval to 1 s with `setPositionUpdateInterval()` and back to 200 ms when the app is shown. Setting it sends a snapshot right away. A second is enough for what still needs the position: the scrobble decision (`ListeningTracker` adds up the position's advance, which stays within its seek tolerance) and the position save. Other state changes (play, pause, track change) are sent when they happen, whatever the interval. The system controls take the position from the Media3 session, not from these snapshots. A new player starts at 200 ms; `AppState` hides a service it creates while the app is hidden.

## Scrobbling

A song is scrobbled once it has been heard for the minimum play time (default 2 min) or the percentage threshold (default 50%), whichever comes first. Both are configurable in Settings. `ListeningTracker` keeps the count for the current play. What counts is the time actually listened (`listened`), not the position: `onPosition()` adds up the position updates while playing, and skips any update where the position moved more than the time that passed (plus a second), i.e. a seek. Otherwise seeking to the end and skipping, or letting the track finish, would scrobble it, and subsoxy would take a skip for a play. When a track completes, the rest after the last position update is counted (`listenedToEnd()`), so a track played through reaches even a 100% threshold. A restored song counts as heard up to its restored position.

Each play is scrobbled at most once: `takeScrobble()` hands out the start time once. A new play starts whenever a song starts (`_setCurrentTrack()` calls `startPlay()`), so replaying a song (e.g. with Previous) scrobbles it again. Completion checks the flag too, so a song that passed the threshold isn't submitted a second time when it ends. A song restored at a position already past the threshold counts as scrobbled, since that happened in the previous session and the queue persisted it.

Requests go through `ScrobbleQueue`, never directly to the API. The queue:

- is stored in SharedPreferences (`scrobble_queue`) and restored on start; an entry that can't be read is dropped on its own, not with the rest
- sends oldest first, 100 ms apart, as soon as a request is queued
- keeps a submission that can't be sent (network error, HTTP error, or the server refusing the credentials) at the front and stops there, so later requests never overtake it. It's retried when the next request is queued, when the network is back (`networkAvailable()`, called by `AudioPlayerService` on the player's network event, the one the downloads use), and by a timer, and dropped only after 7 days. The timer exists only while a submission is blocked: it fires after 30 s, then at twice the delay each time up to 5 minutes, and starts from 30 s again after a send succeeds or the network comes back. With nothing to send there is no timer, so a phone playing with the screen off isn't woken every 30 s for an empty queue. While the server rejects the credentials nothing is sent, and the timer only checks whether that changed.
- calls `onSubmitted` after the server accepted a submission, i.e. counted a play. `AppState` uses it to fetch "Recently played" again, see [CACHING.md](CACHING.md#api-responses-apicache).
- drops a submission the server rejects for itself (a `status="failed"` response that isn't an auth error, e.g. unknown song)
- drops a "now playing" that fails, since it would be stale by the time it's retried. For the same reason `scrobbleNowPlaying()` sends once, without retries (which would also hold up the submissions behind it), and only submissions are saved: a "now playing" restored from the last session would mark a song as playing that isn't.
- is the only retry for submissions: `scrobbleSubmission()` sends once, without the retries of other requests. A submission whose answer timed out may have been counted already. subsoxy drops a repeat of the same song from the same client within half the song's length, but passes every submission on to Navidrome, which counts each one. So a lost answer costs one extra play count in Navidrome at most, rather than one per retry.

subsoxy uses these scrobbles to tell plays from skips, so the queue's ordering and `playedAt` timestamps matter. That's also why `scrobbleNowPlaying()` and `scrobbleSubmission()` throw on failure: the queue has to know.
